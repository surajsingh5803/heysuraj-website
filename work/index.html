WORK / SURAJ KUMAR

Work that connects
ideas to outcomes.

My approach to SEO, performance marketing, measurement, digital experiences and the systems behind sustainable improvement.

BY SURAJ KUMARUPDATED 14 NOV 2026

My approach to marketing starts with the business question rather than the channel. “We need more leads” can mean a visibility problem, an offer problem, an unclear website, a poor-quality audience or a broken follow-up process. The tools are important, but diagnosing the constraint comes first.

Search visibility and on-page strategy

Search is a conversation between a person with a need and a collection of pages competing to meet it. I work across technical checks, Google Search Console, content planning, on-page improvements, internal linking, local SEO and structured data. I try to separate pages that need technical repairs from pages that need a more helpful answer, and pages that are well written but target a query with limited commercial relevance.

A service page for a hospital, for example, is not automatically useful because it mentions a specialty several times. Patients may need to understand what the department treats, which doctors are available, when to seek specialist advice, how appointments work and what information is trustworthy. Content structure and medical accuracy have to work together.

Search Console is especially useful for identifying pages with impressions but weak click-through rates, terms that appear in search without a clearly matching page, and sections that attract informational visits but few meaningful actions. It does not tell the whole story; I pair it with the website journey and business priorities.

Local SEO and business listings

For locally delivered services, Google Business Profiles can be as important as the main website. Categories, completeness, reviews, working hours, service details, photos and consistency between the listing and the site all contribute to a useful local experience.

I also pay attention to what the listing reports actually measure. Calls, website actions, direction requests and profile views can suggest interest, but none is a direct substitute for confirmed appointments or customers. A reporting framework should keep these categories distinct while making the business decision easier.

When several doctors or locations are involved, clarity of identity, address, services and ownership becomes even more important. A listing strategy should help users find the right professional rather than create unnecessary duplicate pages.

Paid acquisition and conversion quality

Google Ads and Meta Ads allow businesses to test demand and messaging more quickly than waiting for organic search alone. But a campaign cannot compensate forever for an unclear service, weak landing page or poorly tracked lead journey.

I look at search intent, creative messaging, landing-page relevance, friction in the form or call journey, and the difference between a platform-reported conversion and a real enquiry. Cost per lead is a useful operational signal, not the only measure of success. If a low-cost lead cannot be contacted, does not need the service or was generated by an accidental interaction, the reported efficiency can be misleading.

In healthcare and other sensitive categories, communication must also remain factual, respectful and compliant with applicable advertising policies. An effective campaign does not require exaggerated claims about outcomes.

Measurement and client reporting

Good reporting explains a decision. It should not bury an important pattern under a wall of screenshots. I work with data from search, ads, analytics, business profiles, forms and website engagement, then try to explain what changed, what may have caused it, what remains uncertain and what should happen next.

A recurring challenge is reconciling overlapping signals. One person may appear as a call click in analytics, a form submission in a CRM and a conversion in an ad platform. Counting all three as unique leads exaggerates demand. A practical report should be explicit about definitions, attribution limitations and data quality.

When businesses can share information about appointment bookings, qualified consultations or revenue, the reporting becomes more valuable. If that information is unavailable, the responsible choice is to state the limitation rather than manufacture an outcome.

Websites and conversion journeys

A website has to help visitors understand and act. I approach website work through information hierarchy, clear calls to action, internal navigation, speed, accessibility, trust and the relationship between mobile and desktop use. A beautifully designed page can still underperform if visitors cannot find the next step.

For service businesses, the details matter: easy-to-find contact information, relevant questions answered near the service description, a clear booking path, and a sense of who is providing the service. For portfolio sites, credibility comes through real descriptions, visible work, and a professional way to get in touch.

Automation and operational systems

I enjoy designing automations for repetitive reporting, lead research, information organisation and workflows that cross multiple applications. My rule is that the automation should be explainable. What triggers it? What data can it access? How are errors handled? What happens when a source is unavailable? Where should a human review the result?

This thinking carries into LP CRM, LP LeadOS and the SEO Audit Engine. They are opportunities to explore how a team might spend less time moving information between tools and more time making good decisions. Their development status is visible because software should not be described as production-ready until it has earned that description.

My working method

1. Define the decision

Before starting work, I try to identify the problem the stakeholder actually needs to solve. Sometimes the question is ranking for a specialist service; sometimes it's improving lead follow-up; sometimes it's explaining a sudden campaign change. Defining success early prevents a lot of wasted activity.

2. Establish a baseline

Choose the metrics and evidence that are actually available. For SEO this may be queries, pages, impressions and indexing status. For campaigns it may be cost, enquiries, quality signals and landing-page engagement. A baseline is not a prediction or a promise; it is a reference for the next decision.

3. Prioritise by impact and effort

Fixing a broken conversion form may be more valuable than publishing another blog. Correcting an important service page may be more useful than making dozens of minor technical changes. The priority depends on business value, confidence, effort, dependencies and risk.

4. Implement and test

Work should be checked where users encounter it: mobile devices, search results, landing pages, calls, forms and reports. The implementation is not complete merely because code was committed or a setting was saved.

5. Explain what changed

A useful review records the result, its limits, what remains unknown and what to test next. Good operational decisions improve when people can revisit the reasoning behind an earlier change.

Industries and environments

My experience has included digital marketing for service-oriented organisations, particularly healthcare and related businesses, as well as exposure to real estate and banking. These environments sharpened my respect for user trust, responsible communication, local intent and the importance of the experience beyond the ad click.

I do not publish private client data or claim outcomes that I cannot substantiate. Public case studies will focus on approved examples, clear methodology and measured results with appropriate context.
