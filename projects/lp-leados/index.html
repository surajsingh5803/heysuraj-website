PROJECT NOTE / ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT

LP LeadOS.

A lead-intelligence and prospect-discovery experiment focused on identifying businesses that may genuinely need a service, with verifiable evidence rather than generic contact lists.

SURAJ KUMARACTIVE DEVELOPMENTLAST REVIEWED 2026-11-14

The problem I wanted to solve

A conventional lead list often answers the wrong question. It can tell an agency which companies exist in a city or category, but existence is not evidence of current need. A business can be perfectly relevant by industry and have no interest in a new provider. Another might be outside the expected sector but actively seeking help with the exact problem a service solves. I wanted a research system that could distinguish business identity from demand signals instead of rewarding whoever collected the longest spreadsheet.

For a consultancy, a useful prospect is a company with plausible fit, evidence that supports the opportunity and a reasonable way to begin a conversation. That evidence might include a public job posting, a relevant project requirement, a campaign signal, a website gap or a request for proposals. None is proof that the company will hire an agency. The output should make this distinction obvious.

Signals, sources and responsible discovery

The research concept explores public websites, business directories, job advertisements, advertising libraries, marketplace requests, industry publications and permitted APIs. The goal is to assemble a grounded picture of what a business is doing, not to scrape private or restricted material. The challenge is that sources vary enormously in availability, freshness, licensing and quality. A record should carry its source, collection time and evidence summary so somebody can verify it later.

A company hiring a digital marketer could be building its internal team rather than considering an external consultancy. A business running search advertising may already have capable support. A low-quality website can suggest an opportunity for improvement but does not establish a budget. These signals can guide qualification and prioritisation, but the model should never treat them as proof of willingness to buy.

Architecture and components explored

The working project uses a web interface, backend processing and a database-oriented workflow. Render and Neon PostgreSQL have been part of the deployment experiments. Brave Search API has been added as one discovery input. A larger design includes request input, provider discovery, result normalisation, deduplication, scoring, evidence display, review and exporting shortlisted opportunities.

Source adapters should remain independently testable so one provider failure does not silently invalidate a whole job. Discovery is only the first stage. Records still require correct identification, business context and contact validation. Search APIs can provide leads to investigate, but they do not automatically produce a qualified, permission-safe outreach list.

What the development process exposed

One recurring difficulty has been the difference between a job displaying “queued” and a system actually producing useful data. The UI can appear busy while a worker is blocked, a provider returns no results or a database state is not updated. These failures are useful lessons in observability: jobs need explicit state transitions, errors, retry limits, provider diagnostics and evidence that results were stored successfully.

Another problem is false-positive targeting. If the search terms describe the agency's services, a naïve system may discover competing agencies instead of customers who need those services. That is a problem of audience definition, not merely crawling speed. The system needs a model of the ideal customer and a separate library of buying signals. Improving relevance is often more valuable than increasing the maximum number of collected URLs.

What a useful result should contain

I would consider a prospect record useful when it identifies the company, explains why it may be relevant, links to verifiable supporting information, avoids duplicates and clearly states what remains unknown. A score can help sort results, but it must be explainable. A number like 90 is not meaningful unless the user knows which observations contributed to it and how trustworthy those observations are.

Contact details should come from appropriate sources and be validated without collecting more personal information than necessary. A research tool should not automatically contact people or imply an endorsement. Human review and compliant outreach are separate stages. This separation makes the system easier to trust and reduces the risk of treating speculation as a business opportunity.

The next engineering priorities

The most useful development tasks are reliable discovery jobs, meaningful error reporting, source quality checks, deduplication and verified exports. Once those basics work, a dashboard can help users filter by industry, location, need, evidence and confidence. More advanced agent research only makes sense once the pipeline records enough evidence to validate its own conclusions.

LP LeadOS remains a living experiment in prospect intelligence rather than a claim that every integration and advanced capability is already operational. The long-term direction is straightforward: fewer irrelevant names, stronger supporting context and a better decision about who is worth contacting.
