ABOUT / SURAJ KUMAR

An evolving story.
Still building.

The people, experiences, experiments and questions that connect my work across business, digital strategy and technology.

BY SURAJ KUMARUPDATED 14 NOV 2026

I didn't begin as a software founder with a tidy origin story. I studied business, participated in marketing and events at college, worked in client-facing settings, and became increasingly interested in the systems behind how organisations attract, serve and retain people. Over time, that curiosity expanded from digital marketing into AI tools, automation and products.

What kind of work excites me?

My professional interests sit where marketing, technology and behaviour overlap. If a hospital website has high impressions but very few qualified enquiries, the answer may lie in page intent, search snippets, site navigation, landing-page trust, or how a patient can contact the team. If a company has a spreadsheet full of leads but a weak pipeline, collecting another thousand names may not help. If an AI product produces impressive output but fails unpredictably, the useful work is in testing and improving reliability.

I like this kind of connected thinking. Instead of treating SEO, advertising, reporting, UX and automation as unrelated jobs, I try to understand the whole workflow: discovery, evaluation, action, follow-up and improvement.

That does not mean I have solved every problem or finished every product. The difference between an idea, prototype and working system matters to me, and I want the site to reflect that honestly.

From business education to practice

I completed a PGDM / MBA at ISB&M Pune from 2022–2024, with dual specialisation in Marketing and HR. My college involvement included the Digital Marketing Cell and Events. Those experiences taught lessons that don't show up in a course module: organising people who have competing priorities, communicating clearly under pressure and actually delivering something on a date.

The business perspective continues to influence how I approach technology. It is easy to build a feature because it is possible; it is harder to decide whether the feature helps a user make a decision or complete a task. Marketing taught me to ask who needs something, why they need it, how they discover it, and what happens once the promise meets reality.

What the different roles contributed

Address Advisors and the real-estate lens

During my real-estate internship at Address Advisors, I was exposed to a sector where customer intent is consequential and the decision cycle can be long. The practical lesson is that buying signals aren't all equal. A person researching a neighbourhood, someone comparing a property type and someone ready to speak with a developer may look like three website visitors, but they need different information and different follow-up.

ICICI Bank and relationship-led work

My time in banking added another perspective: trust, clarity and communication. Financial conversations rarely fit the neat assumptions behind a marketing dashboard. Customer confidence can depend on how a question is answered, how promptly an issue is resolved and how well the relationship is handled over time. That experience made me more sceptical of reporting that counts activity without examining the service experience.

Digital marketing and client work

Across marketing and related roles, I have worked with search optimisation, Google Business Profiles, paid campaigns, analytics, websites, client reporting and content. Healthcare and service businesses are particularly interesting because people are often searching with practical urgency. Search intent, clarity of service information, availability, trust signals and contact paths all influence whether a click turns into a useful enquiry.

In reporting, I distinguish between confirmed enquiries and engagement events. A call click, direction request and completed form are useful signals, but they are not automatically equivalent. I care about separating these interactions before making claims about the outcome of a campaign.

Lazy Plex: the entrepreneurial chapter

Lazy Plex grew out of an interest in building digital solutions rather than only studying them. Starting with services led me to think more seriously about strategy, consulting and systems. The challenge of operating a venture is not just finding clients or delivering campaigns; it is managing expectations, choosing which work deserves attention, clarifying scope, and making the business sustainable.

I continue to view entrepreneurship as a discipline of experiments. A landing page can teach positioning. A proposal can teach communication. A rejected idea can clarify demand. A complicated operational problem can point toward a product. The aim is to learn without pretending every experiment must become a startup.

Today, Lazy Plex Digital is the service-led part of this venture, offering SEO, digital advertising, websites and industry-focused marketing. You can explore its individual services if you are evaluating a marketing project; this personal site covers the wider journey and experiments.

Why I started building tools

Working repeatedly with campaigns and reporting creates a strong temptation to automate what is tedious. That curiosity led to projects such as an SEO Audit Engine, LP LeadOS and LP CRM. They are different systems with different challenges, but share a motivation: making a complex process easier to inspect and act on.

SEO audit engine

A technical SEO audit can surface hundreds of observations. Without prioritisation, that information can be overwhelming. My interest is in connecting the technical condition of a page with actual search demand, visibility, business relevance and the size of the opportunity. A useful audit should help someone decide what to fix first and why.

LP LeadOS

The lead-research project explores discovery signals, prospect fit, evidence, contact accuracy and workflow design. It also raises difficult questions about responsible data collection, false positives and the cost of verification. A large spreadsheet isn't a valuable system unless someone can trust its contents and act on them.

LP CRM

The CRM concept looks at everyday operating tasks: projects, tasks, clients, invoices, time logs, permissions and follow-ups. A comprehensive feature list is easy to imagine; a reliable and comprehensible experience is harder to build. My interest is in the overlap between software architecture and the routines of a real team.

My project archive separates live ventures, experiments, products in development and creative ideas. It is a working record, not an attempt to inflate the number of finished businesses.

Build, think, prove

Three principles sit behind the way I work. Build means trying to create something concrete: a workflow, useful article, working page, prototype or actual campaign. Think means being willing to question assumptions, compare approaches and listen to what users need. Prove means measuring what happened and documenting the limitations of the evidence.

My personal site follows the same principles. It runs on GitHub Pages. The design is interactive but uses straightforward static files, so it is affordable to maintain and readable by search engines. I can iterate in public without needing a complex hosting stack for a personal portfolio.

I am interested in a world where technology makes people more capable rather than more dependent on opaque systems. The more powerful our tools become, the more important judgement, clarity and accountability seem to me.

Outside the day job

My curiosity also extends into content creation, gaming, psychology, writing and visual experimentation. These may not sit under a single job title, and that is fine. A creator learns how attention works; a marketer learns how people discover and evaluate; a builder learns how constraints shape decisions. Ideas cross those boundaries more often than a résumé suggests.

I am working on written ideas around leadership, personal growth and the human side of operating businesses. Some may become long essays or books, others may stay as notes. Either way, they give me a reason to explore beyond purely technical problems.

For a direct conversation, you can also message me on WhatsApp. My Instagram provides a more informal window into what I'm doing.
